\section{System Model and Threat Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{System model}
\label{sec:model:system}

LBVR-Med is a five-layer fabric. The encrypting client (L1) reads each FHIR bundle, splits it into \(\textsf{16~KiB}\) chunks, computes a SHA-256 Merkle tree, and seals every chunk with AES-256-GCM under a per-bundle data-encryption key (DEK) wrapped by a consortium key-management service. The placement orchestrator (L2) applies an RS(2,3) erasure code over the sealed payload, producing two data shards \(D_0, D_1\) and one parity shard \(P_0\), each independently addressable. The retrieval gateway (L3) fetches shards in parallel across tiers and reconstructs via any 2-of-3 surviving shards under a P99 SLO budget. The auditor chaincode (L4), deployed on Polygon zkEVM Cardona, records bundle metadata in \texttt{CIDRegistry}, hosts the PoR challenge/response/verdict protocol in \texttt{PoRVerifier}, and anchors per-retrieval provenance hashes in \texttt{AuditorLog}. The provenance layer (L5) generates W3C PROV-JSON~\cite{w3cprov} per retrieval, signs it with a BLS12-381 quorum, and JCS-canonicalises~\cite{rfc8785} the bytes the on-chain anchor binds. Shards are placed one-per-tier across heterogeneous backends---hot (Pinata dedicated gateway), warm (Filebase S3 + IPFS), cold (Arweave/Irys)---chosen so the fast retrieval path \(\{D_0, D_1\}\) avoids the cold-tier settlement tail entirely.

\subsection{Trust assumptions}
\label{sec:model:trust}

Trusted: the L1 client code (open-source, deterministic), the consortium KMS (HSM-class deployment assumed), the L4 chaincode and the Cardona L2 finality, and the L5 verifier. Untrusted: the storage backends (Byzantine), the network paths between client/gateway/backend, and any individual gateway node below the quorum threshold \(k=2\) of \(N\). The KMS hardening, post-quantum upgrade for BLS12-381, and TEE-attested clients are all named explicitly as out of scope (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Adversary model}
\label{sec:model:adversary}

We characterise three orthogonal capability classes. (i)~\emph{Storage Byzantine}: a controlled fraction \(f\) of pinning replicas refuses to serve, returns corrupted bytes, or disappears. We further distinguish a \emph{uniform} adversary whose behaviour is independent of bundle/shard/caller from a \emph{tier-selective} adversary that targets a specific tier and serves correctly during PoR challenges, degrading silently during real retrievals. The latter is, to our knowledge, not measured in prior decentralized-storage literature. (ii)~\emph{Network attacker}: drops, delays, or partitions on transport; cannot decrypt sealed payloads or forge BLS signatures, but may correlate timing across requests. (iii)~\emph{Curious auditor}: reads on-chain artefacts---bundle hashes, shard CIDs, Merkle roots, PoR verdicts, provenance anchors---and attempts to correlate access patterns with patient identities.
