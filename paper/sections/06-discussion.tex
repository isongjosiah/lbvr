\section{Discussion and Regulatory Alignment}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{EHDS and the EU AI Act.} The European Health Data Space (Reg.\ 2025/327)~\cite{ehds2025reg} requires per-purpose data permits (Art.\ 44--45) and a tamper-evident audit trail for secondary use (Art.\ 47--49). LBVR-Med binds the permit to each bundle via an on-chain \texttt{policyId} hash and produces a BLS-quorum-signed PROV-JSON document per retrieval, anchored on-chain in \texttt{AuditorLog} (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:provenance}); E-PROV's 100\% tamper-detection rate (Table~\ref{tab:eprov-detect}) directly supports the audit-trail claim. The EU AI Act Art.\ 12 record-keeping requirement~\cite{aiact2024} demands automatic logging over the system lifetime: the same provenance pipeline satisfies it, and at zkEVM Cardona gas pricing the per-retrieval anchor cost ($\approx 50$\,k gas) amounts to $\sim$\$0.20/day for a hospital running 10\,000 retrievals/day---affordable even at high throughput.

\textbf{HIPAA Security Rule NPRM (90 FR 898).} The 2025 NPRM~\cite{hipaanprm2025} tightens contingency-plan obligations under \S\,164.308(a)(7). LBVR-Med's RS(2,3) layout satisfies the data-backup-plan requirement structurally (no single provider holds a complete copy), and our measured restore times---137\,ms median for double-data-shard recovery, 8.7\,s P99 for parity-included failure pairs (Section~\ref{sec:eval:erasure})---sit four orders of magnitude under the notional 24-hour restore SLA HHS guidance attaches to high-priority systems. The PoR cadence (30 days per shard) doubles as the periodic-restore-drill testing the same provision requires.

\textbf{Cross-domain applicability.} The cross-SLO calibration (Section~\ref{sec:eval:slo}) places LBVR-Med against five domain SLO regimes spanning four orders of magnitude. The IEC~60601-1-8 alarm budget (10\,s)~\cite{iec60601-1-8} and the IEEE~11073-10700 SDC plug-and-play context~\cite{ieee11073-10700} are met with margin; the IEEE~11073 chart-pull boundary (500\,ms) is the limiting clinical workflow. The Manzi \emph{et al.} interTwin notional 5\,s budget for scientific digital twins~\cite{manzi2025intertwin} is comfortably met. Conversely, the IEC~61850 GOOSE 4\,ms power-grid trip budget~\cite{iec61850goose} is fundamentally infeasible for any decentralized verifiable storage configuration we are aware of---even AES-GCM open + Merkle re-verify costs $\sim$5\,ms on commodity x86. The honest finding here is the load-bearing one: LBVR-Med is suitable for clinical and scientific-DT regimes, unsuitable for sub-10\,ms substation-trip messaging, and the SCADA / asset-management traffic at $\sim$100\,ms--seconds latencies remains an open empirical question for the third paper~\cite{thwe2025dtps}.

\textbf{Scope and roadmap.} Six concerns sit beyond the storage-fabric layer this work characterises, each with a defined forward path. (i)~\emph{Compromised clinician endpoints} are addressed by TEE-attested clients in the journal extension. (ii)~\emph{Side-channel attacks on the consortium KMS} are handled by HSM-class deployment, standard for healthcare key management. (iii)~\emph{Post-quantum security of BLS12-381} is addressed by hybrid signatures targeted for the journal extension. (iv)~\emph{Tier-cooperation collusion} is bounded by the RS(2,3) erasure budget by design; higher-parity schemes are a configurable axis. (v)~\emph{Tier-selective adversary detection gap} surfaced in §V is closed by per-tier PoR-vs-retrieval divergence monitoring at the gateway in the journal extension. (vi)~\emph{Post-retrieval data misuse} is orthogonal to the provenance layer's accountability scope; DLP and downstream access-control are deployment concerns layered above LBVR.
