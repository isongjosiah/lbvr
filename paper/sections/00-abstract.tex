Decentralized storage proposals for safety-critical federated systems---clinical decision-support, scientific digital twins, and power-grid digital twins---are saturated in the literature yet empirically vacant: no peer-reviewed system measures P99 retrieval latency, recovery latency under tier outage, or proof-of-retrievability cadences on realistic workloads. We present LBVR-Med, a verifiable storage fabric for safety-critical federated systems that combines cross-tier Reed--Solomon RS(2,3) erasure across heterogeneous decentralized backends, BLS-quorum-signed PROV-JSON provenance hash-anchored on Polygon zkEVM Cardona, and proof-of-retrievability cadences with auto-migration on consecutive failures. We evaluate the fabric on a 100\,000-patient Synthea corpus (430\,GiB) across thirteen experiments. The fabric meets the IEC~60601-1-8 alarm SLO with margin, attains the IEEE~11073-10700 chart-pull boundary at baseline (P99 565\,ms), and recovers from double-data-shard failure in 137\,ms median via an early-fail short-circuit. We surface a novel ``detection gap'' against tier-selective Byzantine adversaries that hold 100\% retrieval success at $f=0.33$ by passing PoR challenges while degrading silently during real retrievals. Cross-SLO calibration places the fabric against clinical, scientific-DT, and power-grid-DT regimes, identifying which configurations are feasible per domain.
