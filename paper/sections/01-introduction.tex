\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Safety-critical federated systems---clinical decision-support, scientific digital twins, and power-grid digital twins---share a structural need their software stacks have not yet acknowledged: a storage substrate that is simultaneously decentralized (no single jurisdictional or commercial chokepoint), latency-bounded (P99 retrieval inside a domain-specific SLO), and verifiable (a third party can confirm the bytes were retained, who retrieved them, and when). The decentralized-storage literature of 2024--2026 is paradoxically saturated and empirically vacant on this need. Dozens of CP-ABE + IPFS + Ethereum EHR proposals have published in the period, yet a TRL-3 systematic review~\cite{li2025trl} finds the field stuck on synthetic-data proofs of concept, with neither P99 measurements nor cross-tier recovery numbers in any peer-reviewed system.

Two recent results bracket the problem. Trautwein \emph{et al.}~\cite{trautwein2024ipfs} measured public IPFS in INFOCOM~2024 and report PUTs ``taking dozens of seconds to minutes''---a regime that breaks every clinical SLO we are aware of. At the other end, Manzi \emph{et al.}'s interTwin~\cite{manzi2025intertwin} federates five storage backends across eight European countries for scientific digital twins, but publishes neither recovery latency under tier outage nor a proof-of-retrievability cadence; the paper documents a federation rather than a verifiable substrate. Thwe \emph{et al.}'s IEEE Access 2025 review of digital twins for power systems~\cite{thwe2025dtps} positions a similar question for the substation side, identifying data-federation properties as the open research axis. Fang \emph{et al.}~\cite{fang2024metaverse} provide the nearest technical precedent on the medical side---PoR + vector commitments---but neither erasure-code across heterogeneous backends nor measure cross-tier recovery.

This paper reframes the problem as designing and measuring a \emph{verifiable storage fabric for safety-critical federated systems}, evaluated on healthcare as the regulatory motivating case. The architecture is domain-neutral; healthcare is the instantiation because the European Health Data Space~\cite{ehds2025reg} and the EU AI Act Article~12 record-keeping mandate~\cite{aiact2024} provide the clearest retention-SLA pull. We instantiate the fabric---LBVR-Med---as a five-layer system: an encrypting client at L1, a placement orchestrator that performs Reed--Solomon RS(2,3) erasure across hot/warm/cold tiers at L2, a parallel-fetch retrieval gateway at L3, an auditor chaincode on Polygon zkEVM Cardona at L4, and a cryptographic provenance layer at L5 that anchors W3C PROV-JSON~\cite{w3cprov} via BLS-quorum signatures.

We make four contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cross-tier erasure-coded redundancy.} An RS(2,3) protocol that spans three heterogeneous decentralized storage backends (Pinata, Filebase, Arweave/Irys) with measured recovery latencies under both single-tier and double-tier failures at 100K-patient scale.
  \item \textbf{Cryptographically-attested provenance.} An additive extension of W3C PROV-JSON with BLS-signed retrieval receipts, hash-anchored on chain via JCS canonicalization~\cite{rfc8785}, providing tamper-evident data-access histories interoperable with the yProv/interTwin ecosystem and directly supporting the EU~AI~Act Article~12 logging obligation.
  \item \textbf{Cross-SLO calibration.} The first P99 latency measurement for decentralized verifiable storage placed against clinical (IEC~60601-1-8~\cite{iec60601-1-8}), scientific-digital-twin (interTwin), and power-grid (IEC~61850 GOOSE~\cite{iec61850goose}) SLO regimes, quantifying which configurations are feasible per domain.
  \item \textbf{Tier-aware Byzantine withstand.} Measured resilience under uniform and tier-selective adversarial behaviour, surfacing a ``detection gap'' wherein an adversary that targets a single tier and behaves correctly during PoR challenges remains invisible until the per-tier quorum collapses.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} positions LBVR-Med against the closest prior work. Section~\ref{sec:model} states the system and threat models, including the novel tier-selective adversary. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} describes the protocol, including the RS(2,3) layout, the BLS-quorum provenance signing scheme, and the on-chain PoR contract. Section~\ref{sec:eval} presents 13 experiments at 100K-patient scale on Synthea. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} maps the results to EHDS, EU~AI~Act, and HIPAA NPRM provisions. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes with the planned journal extension and a third paper applying the fabric across scientific-DT and power-grid-DT regimes.
