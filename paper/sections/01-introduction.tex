\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Safety-critical federated systems---clinical decision-support, scientific digital twins, and power-grid digital twins---need a storage substrate that is simultaneously decentralized, latency-bounded (P99 inside a domain SLO), and verifiable (a third party can confirm what was retained, by whom, and when). The 2024--2026 decentralized-storage literature is paradoxically saturated and empirically vacant: a TRL-3 systematic review of dozens of CP-ABE + IPFS + Ethereum EHR proposals~\cite{li2025trl} finds none publish P99 retrieval, recovery latency, or PoR cadences on realistic workloads.

Two recent results bracket the gap. Trautwein \emph{et al.}~\cite{trautwein2024ipfs} report public IPFS PUTs ``taking dozens of seconds to minutes''---a regime that breaks every clinical SLO. interTwin~\cite{manzi2025intertwin} federates five storage backends across eight European countries for scientific digital twins but publishes neither recovery latency under tier outage nor a PoR cadence; it documents a federation rather than a verifiable substrate. Thwe \emph{et al.}~\cite{thwe2025dtps} formalise the same gap for power-system digital twins. Fang \emph{et al.}~\cite{fang2024metaverse} are the nearest medical-side technical precedent (PoR + vector commitments) but use a single homogeneous IPFS pool---no cross-tier erasure, no recovery measurements.

This paper presents LBVR-Med, a \emph{verifiable storage fabric for safety-critical federated systems} evaluated on healthcare as the regulatory motivating case. The architecture is domain-neutral; healthcare is the instantiation because EHDS~\cite{ehds2025reg} and EU AI Act Art.\ 12~\cite{aiact2024} provide the clearest retention-SLA pull. LBVR-Med is a five-layer fabric: an encrypting client (L1), a placement orchestrator that erasure-codes RS(2,3) across hot/warm/cold tiers (L2), a parallel-fetch retrieval gateway (L3), an auditor chaincode on an EVM-compatible PoA chain (L4), and a cryptographic provenance layer that anchors W3C PROV-JSON~\cite{w3cprov} via BLS quorum signatures (L5).

We make four contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cross-tier erasure recovery at scale.} The first published measurement of single- and double-tier failure recovery for an RS(2,3) layout spanning three heterogeneous decentralized backends, at 100K-patient corpus scale, including an early-fail short-circuit that drops double-data-shard detection to 137\,ms median.
  \item \textbf{Tamper-evident provenance for high-risk AI.} An additive PROV-JSON extension with BLS-quorum signatures, JCS-canonicalised~\cite{rfc8785} and on-chain hash-anchored, validating EU AI Act Art.\ 12 logging at $\sim$\$0.20/day for 10K retrievals/day with 100\% tamper detection across seven attack categories.
  \item \textbf{Cross-SLO calibration across safety-critical regimes.} P99 retrieval latency mapped against five domain thresholds spanning four orders of magnitude, delineating where decentralized verifiable storage is feasible (clinical alarm IEC~60601-1-8~\cite{iec60601-1-8}, scientific-DT) and where it is fundamentally unsuitable (power-grid GOOSE IEC~61850~\cite{iec61850goose}, 4\,ms).
  \item \textbf{A novel tier-selective Byzantine adversary class.} The first measurement of an adversary that discriminates on tier metadata and serves correctly during PoR challenges, exposing a ``detection gap'' wherein the adversary holds 100\% retrieval success at $f \le 0.33$ despite controlling a third of the targeted tier's replicas.
\end{itemize}
