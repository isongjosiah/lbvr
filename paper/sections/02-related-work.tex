\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Decentralized verifiable storage for safety-critical and regulated workloads has been pursued in three domains that have so far worked in parallel: blockchain-anchored electronic health record (EHR) systems for healthcare, federated digital-twin storage for scientific and power-grid applications, and self-sovereign identity primitives that cut across both. Each domain has produced strong building blocks, but none has produced a substrate that is simultaneously decentralized, latency-bounded inside a domain SLO, and cryptographically verifiable on a measured workload at scale.

The largest cluster targets EHR storage with attribute-based encryption pinned to IPFS and anchored on an Ethereum-style ledger. Li \emph{et al.}'s TRL-3 systematic review~\cite{li2025trl} surveys dozens of such designs and reports that none publishes realistic P99 retrieval, recovery latency, or PoR cadences---the cluster remains empirically thin. The standout exception is Fang \emph{et al.}~\cite{fang2024metaverse}, who pair per-bundle proof-of-retrievability with vector commitments and report on-chain cost numbers; this is the closest published precedent to our PoR layer. However, their substrate is a single homogeneous IPFS pool, so cross-tier failure modes are out of scope by construction and tier-selective adversarial behaviour cannot be exercised on a single backend.

The federated-storage cluster has progressed further on multi-backend orchestration. interTwin~\cite{manzi2025intertwin} federates five backends across eight European countries to support scientific digital twins, demonstrating an operational federation. It does not, however, publish a PoR cadence or recovery latency under tier outage; provenance is handled by yProv, a hash-and-log additive PROV-JSON framework that does not cryptographically sign or on-chain-anchor its assertions. Thwe \emph{et al.}~\cite{thwe2025dtps} formalise data federation and tamper-evident provenance as the open research axes for power-system digital twins, and Savoia \emph{et al.}~\cite{savoia2026fcldt} extend the framing to federated continual learning. These works document federation, not verification.

The remaining literature supplies primitives that our fabric composes but does not compose them itself. The W3C PROV-DM and PROV-JSON Recommendations~\cite{w3cprov} define the provenance data model; RFC~8785 JSON Canonicalisation~\cite{rfc8785} fixes the deterministic byte representation a cryptographic anchor must bind. Mazzocca \emph{et al.}~\cite{mazzocca2025ssi} survey decentralized-identifier and verifiable-credential primitives that underpin gateway identities. Reed--Solomon erasure for fault-tolerant storage is classical~\cite{plank1997erasure}. Trautwein \emph{et al.}'s INFOCOM 2024 measurement~\cite{trautwein2024ipfs} of public IPFS PUT settling time establishes the latency floor that motivates restricting our hot and warm tiers to private dedicated-gateway services. Each primitive is mature in isolation; none is composed into a substrate that delivers all three target properties on a common workload.

Table~\ref{tab:coverage} summarises the coverage of the four Pure-LBVR contribution axes by the closest prior work. The shared limitation is consistent: no published system measures cross-tier erasure recovery, no provenance framework cryptographically signs and on-chain-anchors retrieval receipts, no work calibrates decentralized-storage P99 against multiple safety-critical SLO regimes simultaneously, and Byzantine evaluations assume uniform adversarial behaviour rather than tier-selective attacks that pass periodic PoR while degrading silently between challenges. Pure-LBVR closes each gap with a corresponding contribution---RS(2,3) cross-tier placement with measured single- and double-tier recovery, BLS-aggregated PROV-JSON receipts hash-anchored on chain, cross-SLO calibration, and a tier-selective adversary model---detailed in Section~\ref{sec:method} and measured in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.
