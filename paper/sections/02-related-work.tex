\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Decentralized medical data storage.} The 2024--2026 literature on blockchain-anchored EHR storage is dense but empirically thin. The TRL-3 systematic review of Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{li2025trl} surveys dozens of CP-ABE + IPFS + Ethereum proposals and reports that none publish P99 retrieval latency, recovery latency under storage failure, or proof-of-retrievability cadences on realistic workloads. Among work that exceeds proof-of-concept, Fang \emph{et al.}~\cite{fang2024metaverse} are the closest technical precedent: they pair a per-bundle PoR scheme with vector commitments and report on-chain cost numbers, but the storage substrate is a single homogeneous IPFS pool---neither erasure-coded across heterogeneous backends nor measured under tier-selective adversarial behaviour. The gap motivates contributions (1) and (4): cross-tier erasure with measured recovery, and a tier-selective Byzantine adversary model with measured ``detection-gap'' resilience.

\textbf{Federated storage and scientific-DT provenance.} interTwin~\cite{manzi2025intertwin} federates five storage backends across eight European countries to support scientific digital twins, demonstrating an operational federation but without a verifiable retention property: the paper publishes neither PoR cadence nor recovery latency under tier outage. Provenance in interTwin is handled by yProv, an additive PROV-JSON framework that hashes and logs documents but does not cryptographically sign or anchor them. Thwe \emph{et al.}~\cite{thwe2025dtps} formalise the same gap for the power-system digital-twin community, identifying data federation and tamper-evident provenance as the open research axes for safety-critical regimes. Savoia \emph{et al.}~\cite{savoia2026fcldt} extend the framing to the federated-continual-learning + DT intersection. LBVR-Med's PROV-JSON extension (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) is intentionally additive and remains valid PROV-JSON, so its cryptographic envelopes interoperate with the yProv ecosystem out of the box.

\textbf{Cryptographic provenance and standards.} The W3C PROV-DM and PROV-JSON Recommendations~\cite{w3cprov} provide the data model that LBVR-Med extends; RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization~\cite{rfc8785} provides the deterministic byte representation our on-chain anchor binds. Decentralized-identifier and verifiable-credential approaches (surveyed by Mazzocca \emph{et al.}~\cite{mazzocca2025ssi}) supply identity primitives our gateway DIDs follow but do not by themselves anchor retrieval evidence. C2PA-style content-credentials are adjacent but DICOM-specific, deferred here to journal scope. Reed--Solomon erasure for fault-tolerant storage is classical~\cite{plank1997erasure}; what is novel in LBVR-Med is the cross-tier placement of shards across decentralized backends and the empirical recovery measurement that previous federated systems leave unpublished.

\textbf{Public-IPFS latency literature.} Trautwein \emph{et al.}'s INFOCOM 2024 measurement~\cite{trautwein2024ipfs} of public IPFS PUT settling time motivates LBVR-Med's tiered architecture: the public network is fundamentally unsuitable for clinical SLOs. By restricting the hot and warm tiers to private dedicated-gateway pinning services (Pinata, Filebase) and reserving the cold tier for chain-settled archival (Arweave/Irys), we trade public-network reachability for SLO compliance. Our time-to-availability measurement (Section~\ref{sec:eval}) captures the resulting source-side reachability curve, which is the production-deployment property the paper claims; public-gateway TTA is left to journal scope.

In summary, no published work combines (i) cross-tier erasure-coded redundancy with empirical recovery measurements, (ii) cryptographically-attested PROV-JSON with on-chain anchoring, (iii) cross-domain SLO calibration, and (iv) tier-aware Byzantine evaluation. LBVR-Med is the first system to deliver all four.
